**Choosing the right book is more productive than forcing yourself to read.** Most beginners quit because they select books that don't match their interests, goals, or reading habits. Instead of asking *"What is the best beginner book?"*, ask *"What is the best book for me?"*

## Why Most Beginners Struggle to Read

Many people believe they lack discipline because they can't finish a book. In reality, the problem is usually choosing the wrong book or setting unrealistic expectations.

Reading is a skill that develops over time. Your first goal shouldn't be to complete reading a book—it should be to enjoy reading enough to continue.

# Step 1: Define Why You Want to Read

Before choosing a book, ask yourself:

**Why do I want to start reading?**

People often begin reading because successful people recommend it or because they hear it's a good habit. While that's true, habits only last when they help you achieve something meaningful.

 **Choose a book that either:**

* Solves a real problem.
* Helps you achieve a personal goal.
* Connects with an emotion you're currently experiencing.

### Books That Solve Problems

If you're trying to improve something in your life, start there.

Examples include:

* Concerned about hair loss?    Read a book about hair care.
* Want to build muscle?            Choose a fitness or workout guide.
* Want better communication skills?   Read a communication or social skills book

### Books That Match Your Emotions

Sometimes the best motivation comes from your current interests.

For example:

* In love?   Read a romance novel.
* Interested in fashion?   Read a style guide.
* Struggling in school?    Read the biography of someone who overcame academic challenges.

### A Personal Example

The first book I tried reading was **Relativity**. I couldn't get through even twenty pages in an entire year. 

At that time, I had a crush on a girl.

Later, I picked up **Men Are from Mars, Women Are from Venus** and finished it.

The difference wasn't that the second book was easier.
It was because I had a real reason to read it—I wanted to understand relationships better.

**Lesson:** Choose a book that connects with your current life, not one that everyone else recommends.

# Step 2: Understand Why You Haven't Been Reading

Most people assume they don't read because they're lazy or lack discipline. In reality, that's rarely the case.

The real reason is much simpler: your brain naturally gives priority to tasks that feel more urgent or more rewarding. Unless reading is directly connected to your studies, career, or an immediate goal, it often gets pushed down your list of priorities.

Think about it from your mind's perspective. **If you skip reading today, nothing significant happens.** There are no deadlines, no penalties, and no immediate consequences. For a student preparing for exams or a professional who must stay updated in their field, reading is essential. But for most beginners, reading is simply a hobby they are trying to develop. Naturally, the brain chooses responsibilities that seem more important first.

So don't blame yourself for not always feeling motivated to read. It isn't a character flaw—it's simply how your mind prioritizes competing and fulfilling demands.

Instead of fighting the reasons that keep you from reading, work with them. Make reading fit naturally into your life. Choose books that genuinely interest you, set realistic expectations, and create a reading routine that works around your schedule rather than against it. When **reading becomes convenient instead of demanding,** it transforms from a task you have to do into an activity you genuinely look forward to.

# Step 3: Choose a Genre You Already Enjoy

One of the biggest mistakes beginners make is choosing books simply because they're called "beginner friendly."

A book can be genuinely a good ,easy to read book and still be boring if you don't enjoy its genre.

A better approach is to look at the entertainment you already love.

Ask yourself:

* What movies do I enjoy?
* Which TV shows keep me hooked?
* What kind of stories excite me?

For example:

* Love adventure movies? Try adventure novels.
* Enjoy thrillers? Read mystery books.
* Like fantasy films? Start with fantasy fiction

Today, AI tools can even recommend books according to your favourite movies or TV series.

# Step 4: Set a Realistic Reading Goal

Don't compare yourself with people who claim they finish a book every week.

Every once reading journey is different. Do you think Elon musk read one book a week when he was building his first company in his garage; definitely not, he had more important things at that time.

If you're new to reading, even 10–20 minutes of focused reading can feel mentally exhausting—especially with nonfiction books.

That's perfectly normal.

Reading is a skill, not simply a discipline problem.

Instead of aiming to finish dozens of books each year, choose a timeline that matches your current life. And for most beginners completing your first book in a year is still appreciated. The next year you can definitely double it.

### Example

If you're in your final semester of college, don't expect yourself to finish multiple books outside your coursework.

Choose a realistic goal that doesn't compete with your biggest responsibilities.

# Step 5: Focus on Reading Frequently, Not Reading Longer

This may be the most important advice for beginners.

Don't obsess over finishing books.

Instead, build the habit of opening a book regularly.

Reading five minutes every day is far more effective than reading two hours once every weekend.

If you lose interest in a book, don't force yourself through it.

Try another book that genuinely interests you.

Remember:

If you aim to be a regular reader; Reading should be more as an entertainment than as an education.

If you make it feel like punishment, you'll eventually stop reading altogether.

Consistency builds reading ability.

Enjoyment builds lifelong readers.

# Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best first book for someone who doesn't like reading?

Choose a book that matches your current interests or solves a problem you're facing. Personal relevance is more important than popularity.

### How long should a beginner read each day?

Even five to ten minutes daily is enough to build a consistent reading habit.

### Should beginners read fiction or nonfiction?

Both work well. Fiction is ideal if you enjoy stories, while nonfiction is better if you're trying to solve a specific problem or learn a skill.

### What if I lose interest in a book?

That's normal. Try another book instead of forcing yourself to finish one that doesn't engage you.

### Is it okay to take months to finish a book?

Absolutely. Reading consistently is far more valuable than reading quickly.
